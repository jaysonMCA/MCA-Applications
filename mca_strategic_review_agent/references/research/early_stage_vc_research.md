EARLY STAGE STARTUP SUCCESS SIGNALS AND VC DILIGENCE

Research Window: 2023 to 2026
Research Date: August 2026
Scope: Pre seed and seed venture backed startups, with category overlays for AI, Web3, marketplaces, SaaS, and consumer businesses.

1. Executive Summary

The strongest conclusion from the recent evidence is that the signals that convince a VC to write an initial check are not necessarily the same signals that best predict what happens to the company afterward.

A 2025 NBER study covering more than 8,000 startups sourced by an early stage VC found that team quality was the strongest factor explaining whether a startup subsequently raised at least $1 million of venture capital.

However, team scores became less informative when looking at larger financings and longer term outcomes. Market and product scores carried more explanatory power for companies that later raised $10 million, $25 million, or $50 million.

This creates an important distinction for MCA:

Founder and team quality are extremely important at the beginning, but they should not dominate the entire assessment.

As more evidence becomes available, market quality, product differentiation, customer behavior, retention, distribution, and economics should increasingly drive the review.

Core Findings

Founder and team quality

Founder quality is a powerful initial financing signal, particularly before meaningful company data exists.

MCA should heavily assess founder market fit, execution ability, insight, commitment, access, and complementary capabilities at pre seed.

However, a strong founder story should not indefinitely compensate for a weak product or market.

Market and product quality

Market and product quality appear to become more informative as a company develops.

The review should therefore force analysis beyond pedigree.

Questions should include:

• Is the market genuinely large enough?

• Is the customer problem important?

• Is there a credible reason the opportunity exists now?

• Is the product meaningfully differentiated?

• Can the company realistically capture the market?

Retention

Once a product is operating, retention becomes one of the strongest signals that customers are repeatedly receiving value.

Growth can be temporarily purchased.

Retention is much harder to manufacture.

At seed, retention should therefore carry substantially more weight than at pre seed.

Traction is stage dependent

A pre seed company may reasonably have little or no revenue.

The relevant evidence may instead be customer discovery, technical execution, early users, design partners, prototypes, founder access, or strong problem insight.

By seed, weak evidence of actual customer behavior becomes increasingly difficult to excuse.

Why now

A large market alone is insufficient.

Strong companies often emerge when something changes:

• Technology

• Regulation

• Cost structure

• Distribution

• Infrastructure

• Customer behavior

The review should identify the specific change that makes the company more viable today than it would have been 2 or 3 years ago.

Distribution

Distribution becomes increasingly important once the product exists.

The review should investigate exactly how the company reaches customers, users, suppliers, liquidity, partners, or other participants.

Distribution should not be hidden behind a generic GTM slide.

Capital efficiency

The financing environment from 2023 onward increased investor attention toward capital efficiency.

The most useful question is not simply whether the company burns too much money.

It is:

What uncertainty is being removed with every dollar and month of runway?

AI requires additional skepticism

AI companies can generate exceptional revenue growth and investor demand while still having weak retention, low margins, limited switching costs, or strong dependency on foundation model providers.

High AI growth should therefore be treated as both a positive signal and something requiring deeper validation.

Early stage scoring remains uncertain

No scoring framework can reliably predict venture outcomes.

Scores should organize MCA's thinking.

They should not be presented as probabilities of company success.

2. The Core MCA Principle

The recommended MCA model is:

At pre seed, underwrite the people, insight, problem, timing, and ability to discover the right product.

At seed, increasingly underwrite evidence, including retention, customer pull, distribution, economics, and repeatability.

At both stages, MCA should clearly identify:

• Market quality

• Product quality

• Strategic tensions

• Major assumptions

• Information gaps

• External opportunities

• External threats

3. Funding Signals Versus Outcome Signals

One of the biggest mistakes in early stage evaluation is assuming that something which increases fundraising probability necessarily increases the probability of building a durable company.

Funding is partly a reflection of investor preferences.

Company success is a different question.

Team and Founder Market Fit

Funding signal: Very strong

Evidence for longer term outcomes: Moderate

Pre seed importance: Very high

Seed importance: High

Team was the strongest predictor of initial venture funding in the NBER deal flow study.

However, the importance of team decreased when evaluating larger subsequent financings and longer term outcomes.

Leading seed investors continue to emphasize exceptional founders.

Founder Pedigree Alone

Funding signal: Moderate and noisy

Evidence for longer term outcomes: Weak or uncertain

Pre seed importance: Medium

Seed importance: Low to medium

Elite employers, universities, or previous companies may help fundraising.

They should not automatically be interpreted as founder quality.

The better question is:

What did this person actually accomplish, and why is that experience relevant to this company?

Complementary Founding Team

Funding signal: Moderate to high

Evidence for longer term outcomes: Moderate

Pre seed importance: High

Seed importance: High

Research supports the idea that complementary founding teams can be positive.

This should not become a mechanical rule around founder count.

MCA should look for complementary capabilities across areas such as:

• Technical execution

• Product

• Distribution

• Commercial execution

• Market knowledge

• Operations

Market Size and Timing

Funding signal: High

Evidence for longer term outcomes: Strong

Pre seed importance: Very high

Seed importance: Very high

Market scores appear more informative for larger subsequent financings and longer term outcomes than team scores alone.

MCA should avoid relying on large top down TAM numbers.

The relevant questions are:

• What is the realistic serviceable market?

• How much can the company realistically capture?

• What does the market become if the company succeeds?

• Why does this opportunity exist today?

Problem Severity

Funding signal: High

Evidence for longer term outcomes: Moderate to strong

Pre seed importance: Very high

Seed importance: High

The company should solve a problem significant enough to cause customers to change behavior.

MCA should investigate whether the problem is:

• Urgent

• Frequent

• Expensive

• Risk creating

• Operationally painful

• Already consuming customer resources

Product Differentiation

Funding signal: High

Evidence for longer term outcomes: Strong

Pre seed importance: High

Seed importance: Very high

The product does not simply need to be good.

It needs to be meaningfully different.

MCA should investigate:

• What the company understands that competitors do not

• Why customers choose it

• Why an incumbent cannot simply absorb the feature

• What improves as more customers use the product

Distribution

Funding signal: Moderate at pre seed, high at seed

Evidence for longer term outcomes: Strong practitioner evidence

Pre seed importance: Medium to high

Seed importance: Very high

Product without distribution remains a major early stage failure mode.

MCA should determine exactly where the first:

• 10 customers come from

• 100 customers come from

• 1,000 customers come from

Depending on the model, the same logic applies to users, suppliers, developers, partners, liquidity providers, or marketplace participants.

Early Customer Pull

Funding signal: Moderate to high

Evidence for longer term outcomes: Moderate to strong

Pre seed importance: Medium

Seed importance: Very high

Customer pull can appear through:

• Paying customers

• Repeat usage

• Design partner conversion

• Customer referrals

• Expansion

• Organic demand

• Customers repeatedly requesting the product

Interest should not automatically be confused with traction.

Retention

Funding signal: Medium at pre seed, high at seed

Evidence for longer term outcomes: Strong operational signal

Pre seed importance: Low to medium

Seed importance: Very high

Retention is one of the strongest indicators that users repeatedly receive value.

MCA should prioritize cohort behavior over aggregate growth once enough operating history exists.

Revenue Growth Alone

Revenue growth is useful but incomplete.

Fast growth can be generated through:

• Paid acquisition

• Discounts

• Incentives

• Novelty

• Subsidies

• Unsustainable pricing

The better question is:

What remains after the acquisition pressure is removed?

Unit Economics

Funding signal: Medium

Pre seed importance: Low to medium

Seed importance: High

At pre seed, unit economics may still be theoretical.

At seed, MCA should increasingly examine:

• Gross margin

• Contribution margin

• Net take

• Customer acquisition cost

• Payback

• Revenue sharing

• Infrastructure costs

• Customer concentration

Capital Efficiency

Capital efficiency matters more in the current financing environment.

The correct question is not simply:

How low is the burn?

The better question is:

What milestone does this burn buy, and how much uncertainty does that milestone remove before the next financing?

Business Model and Pricing Power

MCA should ask:

• Who pays?

• Why do they pay?

• How much can they reasonably pay?

• What controls pricing?

• Can a powerful customer or platform compress the company's economics?

• What prevents customers from bypassing the company?

Defensibility

Possible sources of defensibility include:

• Proprietary data

• Network effects

• Switching costs

• Brand

• Distribution

• Regulatory advantage

• Cost advantage

• Workflow ownership

• Exclusive access

The relevant question is:

What becomes stronger as the company succeeds?

4. Team Diligence

At pre seed, the company may have very little measurable operating history.

This makes the founders disproportionately important.

However, MCA should break team quality into specific evidence rather than treating charisma or pedigree as the signal.

MCA Should Ask

Why these founders, for this problem, now?

Look for:

• Relevant domain knowledge

• Direct customer access

• Technical ability

• Commercial ability

• Previous relevant execution

• Recruiting capability

• Speed

• Honesty

• Adaptability

• Complementary capabilities

• Full time commitment

Advisor Quality

Advisor logos should not automatically increase conviction.

MCA should ask:

• Is the relationship contractual?

• Does the advisor have equity?

• How much time do they actually spend?

• What have they concretely done?

• Which introductions or partnerships have they created?

5. Market, Timing, and Problem Severity

Large TAM numbers should be treated carefully.

A more useful analysis includes:

Serviceable Market

What portion of the market can the company realistically serve?

Initial Segment

Which customer segment is most likely to adopt first?

Expansion

What adjacent markets become accessible after the first segment?

Why Now

What changed recently?

Possible catalysts include:

• Regulation

• Technology

• Customer behavior

• Infrastructure

• Distribution

• Cost

• Market structure

• New platforms

Problem Type

The customer problem can broadly fall into 3 categories.

Hair on Fire

The customer already desperately needs the solution.

Demand is clear.

Competition is usually high.

Hard Fact

The problem exists, but customers have accepted it as normal.

The company must convince them to change existing behavior.

Future Vision

The company is building toward a future state customers may not yet understand.

The main challenge is creating belief while surviving long enough for the market to develop.

Different problem types require different diligence expectations.

6. Traction and Retention

At pre seed, a company may have no revenue and still be investable.

Alternative evidence can include:

• Customer interviews

• Prototypes

• Design partners

• Early pilots

• Technical milestones

• Strong founder access

• Signed agreements

• Demonstrated customer pain

Once a product exists, however, MCA should increasingly prioritize actual behavior.

B2B

Inspect:

• Gross retention

• Net retention

• Expansion

• Renewals

• Time to value

• Deployment

• Customer concentration

• Sales cycles

• Contract versus actual usage

Consumer

Inspect:

• Cohort retention

• Daily activity

• Weekly activity

• Monthly activity

• Usage frequency

• Organic acquisition

• Paid acquisition

• Resurrection

• Depth of engagement

There should not be a single universal retention benchmark across all categories.

7. Economics and Capital Efficiency

MCA should distinguish between revenue quantity and revenue quality.

Important factors include:

• Gross margin

• Contribution margin

• Customer acquisition cost

• Payback

• Net revenue retention

• Discounts

• Pass through costs

• Infrastructure costs

• Revenue sharing

• Cash burn

• Runway

For marketplace and transaction businesses, MCA should focus on net take, not headline take.

If the company charges 2.5% but only retains 1% after partners and costs, the economics should be analyzed using 1%.

8. Stage Adjusted Evaluation

The transition from pre seed to seed should be understood as:

Pre seed: underwrite the hypothesis

Seed: underwrite emerging evidence

Series A: underwrite repeatability

Pre Seed

Highest value evidence:

Team

Why are these founders unusually positioned to solve this?

Problem

Is the customer pain real?

Timing

Why now?

Market

Can this become venture scale?

Insight

Does the company understand something non obvious?

Learning

How quickly does the team test and change its beliefs?

Distribution

Where do the first users come from?

Raise

What specific uncertainty does this financing remove?

Seed

Seed requires stronger evidence from reality.

MCA should investigate:

Customer pull

Are customers paying, expanding, referring, or repeatedly returning?

Retention

Do customers stay?

Growth

Is growth repeatable?

Distribution

Are acquisition channels becoming predictable?

Product

Does differentiation survive contact with actual competition?

Economics

Are margins and acquisition economics becoming understandable?

Capital efficiency

Can the company reach the next financeable milestone with current capital?

Market

Can the market support venture scale returns?

Risk

What can realistically kill the company?

9. Current Venture Environment

The 2023 to 2026 venture market has become increasingly concentrated.

Large amounts of capital remain available.

However, that capital is disproportionately flowing toward a relatively small number of companies, particularly AI companies and large high conviction rounds.

MCA should therefore avoid assuming that high aggregate VC investment means fundraising is easy.

The relevant question is:

Can this company clear the bar required for its next financing?

This makes runway, milestone planning, category positioning, and evidence quality more important.

12. Recommended MCA Scoring Model

The score should support the analysis.

It should not replace it.

Pre Seed Weighting

Team and founder market fit: 22%

Problem severity and customer insight: 15%

Market size and timing: 15%

Product differentiation and moat: 12%

Distribution and GTM: 10%

Traction and retention: 7%

Unit economics and capital efficiency: 6%

Business model and pricing: 5%

Competition, regulatory, and geographic risk: 4%

Round, valuation, and milestone logic: 4%

Seed Weighting

Team and founder market fit: 15%

Problem severity and customer insight: 10%

Market size and timing: 12%

Product differentiation and moat: 12%

Distribution and GTM: 12%

Traction and retention: 18%

Unit economics and capital efficiency: 10%

Business model and pricing: 5%

Competition, regulatory, and geographic risk: 3%

Round, valuation, and milestone logic: 3%

Rating Scale

5: Exceptional

Strong stage appropriate evidence that materially reduces risk.

4: Strong

Good evidence with manageable unanswered questions.

3: Plausible

The thesis makes sense but remains substantially unproven.

2: Weak

Meaningful concerns or contradictory evidence exist.

1: Fundamental issue

The underlying thesis may be broken.

Internal Interpretation

80 to 100

High conviction profile.

Prioritize deeper diligence.

68 to 79

Promising but gated.

Identify the 1 to 3 questions that determine whether conviction increases.

55 to 67

Materially unresolved.

Continue only where new evidence, strategic value, or valuation justifies additional work.

Below 55

Weak current case.

Low priority unless a major new fact changes the assessment.

These thresholds should be treated as internal decision support.

They are not probabilities of company success.

13. Confidence Should Be Separate From Score

MCA should report assessment quality separately from evidence quality.

A company may appear extremely strong while the available information remains weak.

Each major conclusion should therefore receive an internal confidence level.

High Confidence

Supported by strong evidence or multiple reliable sources.

Medium Confidence

Reasonably supported but dependent on assumptions.

Low Confidence

Preliminary and requiring further diligence.

If a significant portion of the assessment relies on company provided or low confidence information, MCA should avoid describing the company as high conviction.

14. Hard Overrides

Certain findings should not disappear inside a weighted score.

Integrity Gate

Examples:

• Fabricated traction

• Misrepresented partnerships

• Hidden material information

• Contradictory documentation

Feasibility Gate

The product depends on a technical, economic, or regulatory assumption that credible evidence indicates cannot work.

Commitment Gate

The core team does not have sufficient commitment to execute.

Economics Gate

The business structurally loses money on incremental transactions without a credible mechanism for improving the economics.

Venture Return Gate

Even a strong execution scenario does not produce an outcome large enough relative to the proposed valuation and expected dilution.

A failed hard gate should appear before the numerical score.

16. Red Flags

These can materially damage the investment case.

• Fabricated traction

• Dishonesty

• Unsafe product behavior

• No credible why now

• Product is easily replicated with no compounding advantage

• Economics fail even under favorable assumptions

• Market cannot support a venture scale outcome

• Critical regulatory barrier with no credible resolution

• Fundamental dependency on a party that has no incentive to cooperate

17. Yellow Flags

These require deeper investigation but may be resolved.

• Part time founders

• Warm introductions presented as traction

• Advisor logos without evidence of involvement

• Huge top down TAM with weak serviceable market analysis

• Economics only shown using exceptional customers or transactions

• Weak distribution plan

• Strong dependence on 1 acquisition channel

• Thin take rates

• Dependence on powerful incumbents

• Monetization deferred indefinitely

• Retention not yet measured

• Heavy incentive dependence

• Large partnerships that remain unsigned

18. Evidence Rules for the MCA Skill

Every major point should internally be classified as:

Verified Fact

Externally supported information.

Company Claim

Something stated by the founders, deck, or company materials.

Reviewer Opinion

An MCA team member's judgment.

Research Finding

Something identified through external research.

Inference

A conclusion formed by combining available evidence.

Unknown

Information that cannot currently be established.

Missing information should not automatically become a weakness.

Where possible:

Missing information → explain why it matters → convert it into a diligence question.

The internal framework can be detailed.

The client facing MCA document should remain concise.

The preferred external structure is:

The 2 to 3 strategic questions sitting at the center of the company.

OUR READ

3 to 5 actual strategic conclusions.

Not summaries.

SWOT

Strengths

Internal advantages.

Weaknesses

Internal limitations and concerns.

Opportunities

Threats

6 to 10 questions.

The Prized review should remain the reference standard for this structure.

21. Final MCA Principle

A strong review should not produce 15 equally important observations.

It should determine which 2 or 3 questions everything else depends on.

The purpose of the MCA framework is therefore not to predict which startup becomes a unicorn.

It is to consistently identify:

• What is genuinely strong

• What is still unproven

• What can materially break the thesis

• What external conditions matter

• What evidence would change MCA's view

• What questions deserve to be answered next

That is the standard the review Skill should be built around.

23. Research Limitations

Early stage venture outcomes remain difficult to predict.

The available research has several limitations.

Individual VC datasets may not generalize across all investors, sectors, or regions.

Studies using founder characteristics are observational and should not be interpreted as causal formulas.

Platform datasets such as Carta represent companies visible within those datasets rather than the entire startup universe.

VC firm content is useful for understanding how investors think, but it naturally contains selection and survivorship bias.

There is also limited causal evidence showing that a specific seed stage metric directly causes venture scale success.

The correct MCA output is therefore:

A stage adjusted evidence framework with explicit uncertainty, not a formula claiming to predict winners.
