# VC Review Methodology

The review asks whether the company can become sufficiently large and durable to justify the risk, valuation, and future financing path.

## Information Layers

1. Company narrative: deck, website, company documents, social accounts.
2. Founder narrative: transcript, written answers, follow-up conversations.
3. MCA judgment: reviewer notes and interview answers.
4. External reality: company, competitor, market, regulation, funding, and macro research.

The final assessment should emerge from the interaction between these layers.

## Core Dimensions

- Team: why these founders for this problem.
- Problem: who has the pain, how severe it is, and what they do today.
- Market: serviceable market, initial segment, expansion potential, structure, and pricing power.
- Why now: what changed recently that makes the opportunity newly possible or urgent.
- Product: differentiation, technical difficulty, strategic value, and insight.
- Moat: what gets stronger as the company succeeds.
- Distribution: where the first 10, 100, and 1,000 customers or users come from.
- Traction: distinguish interest, verbal commitment, signed commitment, pilot, paying customer, retained customer, and expanded customer.
- Retention: prioritize repeat behavior when operating history exists.
- Economics: use net economics and include pass-through, incentive, infrastructure, custody, model, payment, and revenue-share costs.
- Capital efficiency: ask what uncertainty is being removed with every dollar and month of runway.
- Competition: include direct competitors, adjacent competitors, incumbents, substitutes, and likely responses.

## Practitioner Lenses From Hash's Playbook

- Start with the venture-return question: whether the company can plausibly produce a large enough outcome at the entry price, not just whether it is a good business.
- Separate ceiling from entry price. A lower entry valuation can make more modest outcomes attractive; a high entry valuation requires a much larger exit after dilution.
- Do not make the blended score the headline. Use sub-scores to identify what would move the company from plausible to high conviction.
- Steelman the company before stress-testing it. A useful review states the strongest bull case and the specific failure modes.
- Separate idea quality from team quality. Either can carry or weaken the case.
- End the analysis with the short list of questions that gate conviction.

## Missing Information

Do not automatically treat missing information as a weakness. Explain why it matters, determine whether it affects the thesis, and convert it into a diligence question.

## Contradictions

Actively identify contradictions between deck, transcript, reviewer judgment, and external evidence. Do not ask a final question that has already been clearly answered in a transcript.

## Strategic Tensions

Each review should identify 2 or 3 central strategic tensions. These are the issues that most determine how MCA should think about the company.

## Category Traps To Check Early

- Marketplace and network plays: cold start, minimum viable density, supply seeding, liquidity subsidy, and disintermediation.
- Neutral protocol versus owned marketplace: neutrality can weaken if the company also competes with its own integrators.
- Non-fungible physical goods: matching by grade or spec can break when the actual item differs on condition, authenticity, or buyer preference.
- Crypto components: determine whether the chain is load-bearing or decorative, and whether wallet, custody, money transmission, sanctions, consumer protection, or securities issues enter the flow.
- Physical-asset models: custody, insurance, authentication, fulfillment, theft, damage, and trust curve are part of the business, not operational footnotes.
